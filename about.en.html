KAI!

Liar’s Dice against a model that calculates, remembers how you play, and records its reasoning before it calls your bid.

How to play

  • Each player rolls five hidden dice. Take turns bidding: “Across the whole table, there are at least N dice showing X.”
  • Every bid must be higher: increase the quantity, or keep the quantity and increase the face. The opening bid must be at least two dice.
  • Ones are wild and count as any face, except during a No-Wilds round.
  • Think a bid is false? Press Call. In a three-player game you may only call the previous bidder. If the bid stands, the caller loses; if it falls short, the bidder loses.
  • The loser drops one die. A player with no dice is eliminated, and the match continues until one player remains.
  • Every player antes one stake at the start of a round, then adds one more after every bid. The winner of the call takes the whole pot, including eliminated spectators’ stakes.
  • Chips are grouped into 1 / 5 / 25 / 100 denominations. Balances carry across matches and may go negative. You can reset your balance at any time, but the reset is recorded in your profile.
  • Every seat keeps a separate model-based balance. Every point in your balance must be won from the opponents at the table.
  • There is no timer. The opponents can see how long you take and remember it.

Declarations

  • Blind: do not look at your dice for the entire round; pot ×2. You may declare it at any point before peeking, even after bidding.
  • No-Wilds: ones stop being wild for this round; pot ×1.5. Only the opening bidder may declare it, before the first bid.
  • Raise: on your turn, double the round’s pot. Each player may do this once per round. The multiplier applies equally to everyone.
  • From the sixth bid onward the table enters Deep Water, automatically doubling the pot again. All multipliers stack.

Why the game is verifiable

  • At the start of every round, each player’s dice and a nonce are sealed into a hash shown on screen. The reveal opens that commitment, so no one can reroll midway.
  • The AI and the human use the same interface and receive the same shape of data. Your hidden dice are not a field in the AI’s observation.
  • Hovering, hesitation, and touch movement before you commit an action are not sampled.
  • System facts are strict; model speech is unfiltered. Results, report data, and objective profile statistics are recomputed by the engine. The opponent may still misjudge, exaggerate, or misremember — that is the opponent speaking, not the referee. You can poke the AI at the table when you disagree.

The machine at the table: a model

  • The model is the opponent. A language model decides how to reason, when to reveal its dice, and whether to call your bid.
  • The prompt contains rules and table data only. It has no persona script, aggression setting, or prescribed voice. Change the model and you change the opponent.
  • We do not filter the model’s table talk. Its own provider or local alignment determines its boundaries. If you connect an unaligned local model with your own key, you are responsible for what it says.
  • Each model keeps its own record. Profiles, balances, and standings are keyed by model name. Hosted and BYOK access to the same model refer to the same opponent.

Before a match, choose head-to-head or a three-player table and select the opponents. Each card shows that model’s record against you.

Connecting a model

  • No setup (default): the first lobby seat is hosted. Start immediately; its usage is covered by the game while the shared service is available.
  • Access code: enter it in Lobby Settings to unlock the other official model seat and its quota.
  • Bring your own API: configure Base URL / API Key / Model on the Guest Seat card. OpenAI-compatible and Anthropic formats are supported. An OpenRouter URL can load its current model catalog. Your key stays on this device; the browser connects directly to the provider.
  • No available model channel: the opponent still plays using the silent mathematical fallback. On a call, it gives a short fact-based reason.

About model names

  • Model names in the game are factual labels so you can identify and verify which model is connected to a seat.
  • This game is unofficial, unaffiliated with model providers, and not endorsed by them. It uses none of their logos, trademarks, or mascot imagery.
